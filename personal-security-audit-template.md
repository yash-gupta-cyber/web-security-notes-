# Personal Security Audit Report
## Overview
This document represents a structured approach to performing a personal digital security audit. It is designed for learning purposes and demonstrates how to identify risks, assess exposure, and plan remediation.

---

## 1. Scope

Systems and assets assessed:
- Primary email: [example@email.com]
- Key accounts: [Google, social media, banking, developer platforms]
- Devices: [mobile, laptop, desktop]
- Network: [home Wi-Fi / public networks]

---

## 2. Executive Summary

The assessment identified multiple areas of improvement in account security and access control. The primary risks include weak authentication practices and unnecessary third-party access. Immediate actions were taken to strengthen authentication and reduce exposure.

---

## 3. Findings

### Critical
- Missing multi-factor authentication (MFA) on primary accounts
- Password reuse across multiple services

### High
- Exposure from historical data breaches
- Active sessions on unknown or unused devices

### Medium
- Excessive third-party application access
- Public exposure of email across multiple platforms

### Low
- Inactive or forgotten accounts still accessible
- Old accounts linked to outdated email addresses

---

## 4. Actions Taken During Audit

- Enabled multi-factor authentication (MFA)
- Logged out of unrecognized sessions
- Revoked access to unused third-party applications

---

## 5. Remediation Plan

### Short-Term (This Week)
- Enable MFA on all critical accounts
- Update weak or reused passwords

### Mid-Term (This Month)
- Set up a password manager
- Review and remove inactive accounts
- Audit account recovery options

---

## 6. Key Learnings

- Strong authentication significantly reduces risk
- Account exposure often comes from overlooked services
- Regular audits are essential for maintaining security

---

## Conclusion

A structured approach to personal security helps identify weaknesses early and ensures continuous improvement in digital safety practices.
